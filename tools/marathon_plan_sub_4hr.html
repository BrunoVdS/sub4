♥ Heart rate
You’ve had a cardiac ablation and your HR no longer climbs the way it used to — your data confirms it. Over the last ~10 months your running HR peaks at 160–165 for only a few seconds, and your best sustained 20-min HR is ~152 (set in your half marathon). That’s a compressed range, so chasing HR numbers is misleading. Drive this plan by pace and perceived effort; treat HR only as a ceiling, not a target. The figures above are re-based on your real data — not the max-200 in your TrainingPeaks profile, which is wrong for you now and inflates every zone. Because HR won’t spike to warn you (heat, fatigue), lean on RPE and how your legs feel instead. Get clearance from your cardiologist/EP that sustained threshold and marathon-pace efforts are fine and whether they want a hard HR cap — their number overrides anything here. Stop and get checked for palpitations, chest pain, unusual breathlessness, dizziness or fainting.
04 How to execute
What the session labels actually mean and how to run them. Paces are a guide — on a hot day or tired legs, run the effort, not the watch. And with your capped HR (see above), pace + perceived effort lead; HR is only a ceiling.
Strides~3:30–3:50/km feel
Short, relaxed accelerations of 15–25 s — not sprints. Build smoothly from a jog to about 90–95% effort over the rep, holding tall, loose form with quick turnover, then float down. Walk or jog 45–60 s between each. Run them on flat ground after easy runs, or as part of a warm-up before a quality session. They sharpen your legs and running economy and prime faster turnover without adding fatigue.
Float recovery~6:00–6:15/km
The easy jog between hard reps (e.g. “1k float”). Keep moving — don’t stop — let your breathing settle and heart rate drop, then start the next rep. The float is part of the workout, not a break.
Warm-up & cool-downWU / CD
WU: 2–3 km easy building to steady before any quality work, plus a few strides — it lets you hit target pace from the first rep and protects against injury. CD: 1–2 km very easy jog afterwards to flush the legs.
Easy / Recovery@5:45–6:00 / @6:00–6:15
Conversational — you could hold a full sentence. This is the bulk of your running and it builds the aerobic base. If in doubt, go slower; easy days must be genuinely easy so the hard days land.
Steady / LSD@5:25–5:40
A notch above easy: controlled, rhythmic, still aerobic. Used for long-run base pace and steady efforts. Breathing is present but unforced.
Marathon pace (MP)@5:38–5:43
Your goal race pace (5:41/km). In early weeks it should feel almost too easy — that’s correct. The skill is grooving it so it feels automatic. HR sits ~142–148. The whole block teaches your legs to hold this for 42 km.
Threshold (T)@4:55–5:10
“Comfortably hard” — roughly one-hour race effort. Breathing is rhythmic and controlled; you can say a few words but not chat. HR ~150–155, near your capped ceiling — let pace, not HR, define it. This lifts the ceiling so MP feels easier.
Intervals (I)@4:35–4:50
10k-to-5k effort — strong and repeatable, not a sprint. Used sparingly to keep top-end speed and economy. Run by pace, not max HR. You should finish the set feeling you could have done one or two more reps.

06 Recovery · weeks −3 to −1
Before Week 1 — the three weeks straight after IM Tours (15 Jun – 5 Jul). These are for absorbing the Ironman, not training. You won’t lose fitness in three weeks and your base is huge; the only goal is to reach 6 Jul fresh and uninjured. Spend energy on low-impact movement, not training stress.
−3 · 15–21 Jun
Recover

Near-total rest. Sleep, eat properly (don’t under-fuel), hydrate, daily walks. No running. Restless by the weekend? One or two very easy short spins or a loose swim — that’s all.

−2 · 22–28 Jun
Easy aerobic

Easy spins, easy technique swims, walks/hikes, mobility — all by feel. Good moment to start the no-buoy swim + kick groundwork. Tiny easy jogs only if the legs feel 100%.

−1 · 29 Jun–5 Jul
Prime

A little more easy volume: longer easy rides, a couple of relaxed 20–40 min runs, a few strides at the very end of the week. In reality July stayed quiet — the three weeks that followed are logged below as they happened, and Wk 1 now starts 27 Jul.

Good outlets
Easy bike / Zwift, swimming, walking & hiking, SUP / kayak, mobility & yoga. Low impact, low intensity, enjoyable.
Avoid
Running early on trashed legs, any threshold / intervals / hard long rides, heavy lifting, racing — nothing that creates fresh muscle damage or deep fatigue.
♥ For you
With your capped HR, don’t judge recovery by HR or HRV — go by sleep, motivation, leg soreness and how easy pace feels. A post-IM immune dip is normal, so bank extra sleep. Use the downtime for the admin: get EP clearance for the block, fix your TrainingPeaks max-HR/threshold + rTSS, and book a fresh running test if you want one.

08 Swim block

Swimming is maintenance here, not a focus — one session a week, capped at 1200–2000 m. Pool is 50 m, so 25 m items don’t exist here: anything short is swum as a 50 — first 25 kick or drill, second 25 regular swim. Two goals: keep your feel for the water, and get off the pull buoy so your core and legs hold your body position instead of the float doing it for you.
NO BUOY
You’ve been leaning on pull buoy + paddles for most of your volume. This block removes the buoy entirely. Expect your hips to sit a little lower at first — that’s normal, and it’s exactly what the kick progression below is for. Paddles can stay occasionally (without the buoy) for catch strength, but the default is plain swimming — warm-ups included. Early on, break the WU into 50s with short wall rests rather than reaching for the buoy; the split is temporary, the buoy would be a step backwards.
Building the legs — kick progression
1Wk 2–3
Wake the legs up

Short, easy kick in 50s: 4×50 (25 kick / 25 swim) → 6×50 full kick on a board — the swim half of each 50 is recovery, so the kick dose starts small on purpose. Cue the kick from the hip, not the knee, with loose ankles, pointed toes and a small, fast amplitude — just enough to lift the hips. Optional short fins to feel the ankle snap. The point is feel, not effort.

2Wk 4–6
Build & integrate

More volume (6–8×50) and start tying the kick to the stroke: the 6-kick-switch drill links it to your body roll. Begin holding a light, steady kick through full swims — a 2-beat to keep distance relaxed, a 6-beat when you need to lift the hips.

VWk 7–10
Vacation gap

No scheduled swims on the trip. If you do swim, keep it easy and technical — don’t chase the progression. Pick it back up on your return.

3Wk 11–17
Rebuild + body-position engine

Ease back in after the gap, then carry a steady kick on every swim — with no buoy it’s what keeps you flat. Maintain 6–8×50, a few faster 50s for leg endurance. Keep it aerobic on heavy run weeks.

4Wk 18+
Maintain

Drop to 4–6×50, easy. Keep the feel, add nothing. Fresh legs win the marathon.

08b Strength block

New in Rev 4. Two short sessions a week, ~65 min total — run, ride and swim volumes are untouched and strength never appears in the km/h totals. The case: heavy, low-rep strength work plus a small dose of jumps improves running economy by roughly 2–4% and about halves overuse-injury risk (Blagrove 2018; Balsalobre-Fernández 2016; Lauersen 2014) — and at your body mass, with the long run climbing to 34 km, the injury protection is worth more than the economy. What works is heavy and crisp, never to failure — not high-rep circuits.

10 Race day · eat & drink

Target 5:41/km = 4:00:00, fuelling ~80 g carbs/hr — well within what your IM gut already handles. Everything below must be rehearsed on the Wk 32 race-sim long run (and previewed on the Wk 17 peak long run).
Before the race
  • 2–3 days out: carb-load ~7–8 g/kg/day from familiar carbs; top up between meals with Leppin. Normal sodium, stay hydrated.
  • Day before: high-carb, low-fat / low-fibre meals; nothing unusual.
  • 3 h before: familiar breakfast ~1.5–2 g/kg carbs (e.g. white bread + jam/honey, banana) + 500–600 ml fluid.
  • 15 min before: 1 ULTRA gel (45 g) + a few sips of water.

Hydration, salts & pacing
Hydration
~400–500 ml/hr for a cool March race. Leppin carries ~300 mg sodium per 500 ml; add a pinch of salt or an electrolyte tab if it’s warm. Drink to thirst, small and often.
Pacing
First 5 km controlled 5:43–5:45, settle to 5:39–5:41, hold. Halfway ~2:00:00. The race is won by not slowing after 32 km — aim 3:58–3:59.
♥ On the day
Nothing new — only what you rehearsed. Caffeine gels only if EP-cleared. If your gut rebels, drop to water + Leppin and resume gels once it settles. Stop and seek help for chest pain, palpitations, dizziness or fainting.

11 Tracking & adjusting

How to read this plan against what you actually run (TrainingPeaks / Strava), and change course without breaking the build. The per-week km + time are your targets; the session colours are the intent. Compare reality to both.
Did it happen?
Sessions completed vs planned — 3 runs, the long run, the bike, the swim. A single miss is nothing; a pattern of them is the signal to back off.
Did easy stay easy?
The #1 check for a Z2 base. Easy and steady runs held at easy effort — not drifting fast. Easy-run-too-fast is the classic way to quietly wreck an aerobic block.
Long-run durability
Did pace hold through the back third at the same effort? On the race-sim, was MP holdable to the finish? This is exactly what the base is built to deliver.
Volume vs target
Weekly km / time near the target band. Chronically scrambling to hit it → target’s too high. Always well under → you’re fresher than planned, or missing work.
The metric that matters
Aerobic efficiency. Over the weeks, the same easy pace should feel easier and sit at a lower effort — that is the Z2 base growing. Because your HR is capped and blunted, lean on pace-at-RPE and how long you hold easy pace before form decays, not raw HR. Once the December test re-sets your zones, pace:HR decoupling across a long run (drift under ~5% = well-paced and aerobically fit) becomes a clean monthly read.
